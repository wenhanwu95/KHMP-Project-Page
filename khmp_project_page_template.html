Abstract

Stochastic human motion prediction must model both uncertainty and temporal realism, yet existing methods often suffer from high-frequency jitter and biomechanically implausible motion artifacts. KHMP addresses these issues through a dual strategy: structured physical constraints during training and adaptive Kalman refinement in the DCT frequency domain during inference. The result is improved motion fidelity, smoother trajectories, and strong quantitative performance on HumanEva-I and Human3.6M.

Why KHMP?

This section should communicate the paper’s problem setting in a visual and intuitive way before readers dive into the method.

Challenge 1: High-Frequency Jitter

Existing stochastic predictions can exhibit temporal discontinuities and noisy motion oscillations that degrade realism.

Challenge 2: Physical Implausibility

Frame-wise objectives alone do not fully enforce biomechanical structure, often leading to implausible poses or bone deformation.

Core Insight

Treat high-frequency DCT coefficients as a structured sequence and apply adaptive Kalman filtering to suppress noise while preserving motion detail.
